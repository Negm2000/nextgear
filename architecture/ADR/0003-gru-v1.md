**ID**: `cpt-nextgear-adr-gru`

## Context and Problem Statement

Many rejections or repairs can separate an accepted gear from a later decision that depends on it. A fixed window of recent events can lose that information.

## Decision Drivers

- Raw event inputs
- Training on a CPU
- A measured benefit over simple alternatives

## Considered Options

- One-layer GRU
- Interpolated n-gram
- Transformer or graph model

## Decision Outcome

Chosen option: "One-layer GRU". It is a practical first learned model for short discrete sequences. Its value has to be shown by the comparisons, not by novelty.

### Consequences

PyTorch becomes a dependency. The model learns observational probabilities and fails the system checks of the excluded combination, so no general architectural reasoning is claimed.

### Confirmation

Compare every configured seed with the validation-selected n-gram and the engineered state counts. Report scores on held-out, novel-trace, changed-agent and excluded-group sessions.

## Pros and Cons of the Options

### One-layer GRU

Learns a recurrent summary of the history at modest CPU cost, but varies across seeds and transfers poorly to unseen combinations.


### Interpolated n-gram

Fast and interpretable, but sees only a short window; kept as a baseline.


### Transformer or graph model

More expressive, but they need more tuning and data, and nothing here shows the extra capacity is needed.
