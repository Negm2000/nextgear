**ID**: `cpt-nextgear-adr-gru`

## Context and Problem Statement

Accepted gears can be separated from later decisions by many rejections or repairs. A fixed event window may lose this state.

## Decision Drivers

- Raw event inputs
- CPU training
- Measured benefit over simple alternatives

## Considered Options

- One-layer GRU
- Interpolated n-gram
- Transformer or graph model

## Decision Outcome

Chosen option: "One-layer GRU". It is a practical first learned baseline for short discrete sequences; its value is established by comparisons, not model novelty.

### Consequences

PyTorch is an added dependency. The model learns observational probabilities and fails the excluded-combination system-check test; no universal architectural reasoning is claimed.

### Confirmation

Compare all configured seeds with validation-selected n-grams and engineered state counts. Report IID, novel-trace, policy-shift and group-exclusion scores.

## Pros and Cons of the Options

### One-layer GRU

Learns a recurrent summary with modest CPU cost; seed variation and poor compositional transfer remain.


### Interpolated n-gram

Fast and interpretable, but limited history; retained as a baseline.


### Transformer or graph model

More expressive options, but add tuning and data demands without evidence of necessity here.
